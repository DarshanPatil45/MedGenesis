## Overview

Diabetes is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated levels of
blood glucose, which over time leads to serious damage to the heart, blood
vessels, eyes, kidneys and nerves. The most common form is type 2 diabetes,
usually occurring in adults, which happens when the body becomes resistant to
insulin or does not make enough insulin.

## Global burden

The number of people living with diabetes worldwide has risen substantially
over recent decades, with the sharpest increases in low- and middle-income
countries. A large share of people living with diabetes are undiagnosed, and
many who are diagnosed are not receiving treatment. Diabetes is a leading
cause of blindness, kidney failure, heart attacks, stroke and lower limb
amputation, and is a significant direct cause of death globally.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of diabetes may include feeling very thirsty, needing to urinate
more often than usual, blurred vision, feeling tired, and losing weight
unintentionally. Symptoms of type 2 diabetes can be mild and may take years
to be noticed, which is why many cases are found only when complications have
already developed. Anyone experiencing possible symptoms should be assessed
by a health professional.

## Prevention

Type 2 diabetes can often be prevented or delayed. Measures shown to help
include reaching and maintaining a healthy body weight, being physically
active with at least 30 minutes of moderate exercise on most days, eating a
healthy diet with reduced sugar and saturated fat intake, and avoiding
tobacco use, since smoking increases the risk of diabetes and cardiovascular
disease.

## Treatment and management

Management of type 2 diabetes centers on a healthy diet, physical activity,
and maintaining normal body weight, alongside blood glucose monitoring and,
where needed, medicines prescribed and supervised by health professionals.
Access to affordable treatment, including insulin for those who require it,
is critical to survival for people living with diabetes. Regular screening
for complications of the eyes, kidneys and feet supports earlier
intervention.

## Diagnosis and screening

Early diagnosis can be achieved through relatively inexpensive blood glucose
testing. Diagnosis and any interpretation of test results are the
responsibility of qualified health professionals. Population-level screening
recommendations differ between countries and health systems.
